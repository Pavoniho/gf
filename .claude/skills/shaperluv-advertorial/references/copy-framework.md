# Copy framework

The layout is fixed; the copy is what you actually write. This is the structure the
original follows, with its own lines as the worked example.

## Headline

> **N REASONS WHY [PRODUCT] [BIG PROMISE] WITHOUT [THE THING THEY DREAD].**

The original: *5 REASONS WHY SHAPERLUV INSTANTLY TRANSFORMS YOUR PHYSIQUE WITHOUT GOING TO THE GYM.*

Three parts, in this order:
1. The number — sets the reader's expectation of a short, skimmable page
2. The transformation, stated as already-done (`INSTANTLY TRANSFORMS`, not `can help you transform`)
3. The escape clause — the effort, cost, or risk the reader is dodging (`WITHOUT GOING TO THE GYM`)

All caps. One sentence. Ends with a period.

## Hook paragraph

> **[Pain, phrased as a question they'd answer yes to] Keep reading to find out how [PRODUCT] offers [the shortcut] to [the outcome].**

The original: *Tired of feeling self-conscious about your belly in public? Keep reading to find out how Shaperluv offers an instant, invisible solution to look fit and feel confident.*

Two sentences, never three. The first names the feeling, not the product. The second is a
literal instruction to keep reading — it works because it tells the reader the page is short.

## The five reasons

They are not five features. They are five objections answered in order:

| # | Job | Original |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The promise, made physical.** Lead with the transformation itself. | *Go from fat to fit instantly* |
| 2 | **"Will people know?"** Kill the embarrassment objection. | *Invisible under your clothing* |
| 3 | **"Will it hurt / be annoying?"** Kill the comfort objection. | *Feel sculpted, not squeezed* |
| 4 | **"Will it last?"** Kill the durability / value objection. | *It won't stretch out and it's made to last* |
| 5 | **"Am I the only one?"** Social proof with a number. | *Join the legion of confident men* |

If you write more or fewer than five, keep the sequence: promise → discretion → comfort →
durability → belonging. Cut from the middle, never from the ends.

### Reason titles

- Numbered, `1.` through `5.`
- 3–8 words. Reason 4 in the original runs long on purpose and gets the smaller type ramp
- Written as a claim, not a feature name: *Feel sculpted, not squeezed*, not *Comfort fit*

### Reason body

One to three sentences. Never a paragraph. Each one does benefit-first, mechanism-second:

> *The powerful compression technology works like magic, immediately sculpting your physique
> as if you've been working out for years.*

Techniques the original uses, worth stealing:

- **Invented proprietary tech names** — *FormRetain Tech*, *compression technology*. A named
  mechanism reads as engineered rather than generic.
- **Absurdly specific numbers** — *Wear it 89 times and it'll still be as elastic as day one.*
  89 is more believable than 100 precisely because it isn't round.
- **One joke, late.** Reason 4 ends: *Guaranteed to last longer than the girl you met at the bar.*
  Exactly one line of humor, placed after trust is built, never in reasons 1–2.
- **A membership number in the close** — *over 25,000 satisfied men*. Reason 5 is always
  the crowd.

## Product section heading

> **BEST SELLER - [ONE-LINE PRODUCT PROMISE]**

The original: *BEST SELLER - PERFECT APPEARANCE IN ANY OUTFIT*

All caps, hyphen-separated, centered above the product. The `BEST SELLER` half is fixed;
the second half restates the core promise in five words or fewer.

## Product description

> **Introducing [PRODUCT], [what it is] meticulously crafted to [do the thing]. This unique
> product is designed to [secondary benefit].**

Two sentences. Bold the product name. The original bolds *SHAPERLUV™* and
*chest and mid-section* — bold the product and the body part or problem area, nothing else.

## CTA text

`BUY NOW`, all caps, on all three buttons. Don't get creative here — the sticky bar has
room for two words and the reader has seen it for the whole scroll.

## Voice rules

- Second person throughout. *You*, *your*. Never *customers* or *users*.
- Present tense for the transformation. It already happened.
- No hedging: no *can*, *may*, *helps to*. The original says *works like magic*.
- Short sentences. If a line runs past two commas, split it.
- Claims stay on the product's own terms — comfort, appearance, durability, confidence.
  Don't drift into medical or health claims; the format doesn't need them and they create
  a problem the page can't solve.
